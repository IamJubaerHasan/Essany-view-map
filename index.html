Industry pages and their sub-pages

Keywords are suggested targets based on how buyers usually search. Search volume and difficulty have not been checked yet.

What every industry page includes

1

Their money problem, in their numbers. Referral fees for senior living, unused co-op funds for equipment dealers, slow lead response for marine.

2

Our services for that industry. What we do for them and what changes.

3

Proof. Case studies, results and client logos as we win clients in each industry.

4

Industry FAQs. Real buyer questions; also helps AI search and answer engines.

5

One clear call to action. For example a free website and tracking audit.

Segment sub-pages

Same layout as the industry page, but written for one buyer group. Memory care families, messaging and rules differ from independent living; a crane dealer sells differently from a farm equipment dealer.

/industries/senior-living-marketing/memory-care/

Location pages: later, and only with proof

  • These are national B2B niches. Searches like "senior living marketing agency Texas" exist but are small.
  • Add a state or city page only where we have a client, a case study or real local knowledge (state senior-living rules, major dealer markets).
  • Google treats near-identical city and state pages as doorway pages, which can hurt the whole site. Every location page needs unique content.
/industries/senior-living-marketing/texas/

Build order

  1. Check keyword volume and difficulty for every page.
  2. Build the /industries/ hub and the 7 core industry pages.
  3. Add segment sub-pages, starting with the industries we pitch first.
  4. Add case studies as clients come in, then location pages where proof exists.
  5. Decide on the 2 optional industries after the core pages are live.
